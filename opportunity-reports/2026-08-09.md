## Opportunity Reviews

### LangGraph checkpoint persistence and cost diagnostics
- Observed demand signal: stateful LangGraph users keep hitting persistence correctness and checkpoint cost issues in production-like paths.
- Evidence sources and dates: #7714 (2026-05-05), #7263 (2026-04), #5790 (2025-07-31), #5672 (2025-07-25), and #7417 (2026-04-05).
- Repeat pattern: the same family of bugs spans storage bloat, replay loss, ignored checkpointers, and duplicate execution after long-running work.
- Likely user or buyer: teams building production assistants, workflow agents, or internal automation on LangGraph Cloud or self-hosted checkpointer stacks.
- Current workaround or money signal: custom checkpointers, Postgres/SQLite saver selection, LangSmith debugging, and third-party integration packages.
- Why existing solutions appear insufficient: the issues show that persistence and replay guarantees are still easy to violate or make inefficient in default paths.
- One-week test: publish a free benchmark/diagnostic CLI that measures checkpoint size, query count, replay fidelity, and duplicate-run risk across LangGraph saver setups.
- Falsification threshold: if users cannot reproduce the pain on real deployments or the benchmark shows negligible cost/correctness differences across common savers.
- What should not be built: a new orchestration framework or a replacement agent runtime.
- Confidence: medium-high, with the main gap being spend data and a clearer count of production deployments.

### Tailscale split-tunnel routing exception diagnostics
- Observed demand signal: users repeatedly want domain-, app-, or device-specific exceptions to exit-node and DNS routing behavior.
- Evidence sources and dates: #6912 (2023-01-08), #13677 (2024-10-03), #15521 (2025-04-03), #3506 (2021-12-05), and #18378 (2026-01-10).
- Repeat pattern: the same routing pain shows up across Android, macOS, exit nodes, local DNS, and regression reports after updates.
- Likely user or buyer: IT admins, privacy-minded power users, and small-fleet operators who mix Tailscale with other VPNs or local networks.
- Current workaround or money signal: disable MagicDNS, use App Connector, route through another VPN, or exclude the whole browser/app instead of only the destination domain.
- Why existing solutions appear insufficient: the workarounds are coarse, fragile, or platform-limited, and they do not match real browser-and-site usage.
- One-week test: publish a routing policy linter or decision helper that explains whether a Tailscale setup will need split tunneling, App Connector, or DNS overrides.
- Falsification threshold: if most users are satisfied with the current app-level workarounds or if the pain is limited to edge-case hobby setups.
- What should not be built: a generic consumer VPN client or another full network stack.
- Confidence: medium, with the main gap being direct evidence of budget attachment for a dedicated diagnostics product.

### ArduPilot telemetry integrity and failsafe diagnostics
- Observed demand signal: operators and developers repeatedly report telemetry loss, logging gaps, and unreliable failover detection in the flight stack.
- Evidence sources and dates: #9752 (2018-11-10), #27746 (2024-08-03), #28807 (2024-12-05), #32351 (2026-03-02), and #32905 (2026-04-24).
- Repeat pattern: packet loss, lost ESC telemetry, missing log records, and unreliable telemetry start-up all recur as separate reports.
- Likely user or buyer: UAV operators, integration shops, robotics labs, and developers who use Mission Planner or similar GCS tools.
- Current workaround or money signal: manual log inspection, firmware changes, power-cycling, and relying on GCS stats windows to spot problems.
- Why existing solutions appear insufficient: current tooling reports symptoms but does not give a clean reliability score or actionable integrity view for telemetry streams.
- One-week test: publish a log analyzer that flags packet loss, ESC telemetry gaps, and DShot reliability problems from ArduPilot logs.
- Falsification threshold: if the failures are mostly one-off hardware wiring issues and not a recurring software-operational gap.
- What should not be built: autopilot firmware, control laws, or a replacement ground-control station.
- Confidence: medium, with the main gap being direct procurement evidence.
